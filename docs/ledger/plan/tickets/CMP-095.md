# CMP-095 — An owner of a customer Organization cannot grant into another Organization

## In plain words

**The problem today.** The policy that keeps a grant inside its own Organization lets any owner grant into any other Organization: it looks at the requester's role and never at the requester's Organization. Its own description and message say that only an owner of the management Organization may grant across Organizations, and its test suite expects the wrong verdict.

**What changes.** A grant into another Organization is refused unless the requester belongs to the management Organization and is its owner. The suite gains an owner and a developer of the management Organization, and an owner of a customer Organization is refused across Organizations and still admitted inside its own.

**Done means:**

- An owner of a customer Organization who grants access into another Organization is refused by the policy.
- An owner of a customer Organization who grants access inside the own Organization is still admitted.
- An owner of the management Organization may grant into any Organization; a developer of the management Organization may not.
- The suite's copy of the policy is the claims chart's render, byte for byte, and its twenty tests pass.
- A Sovereign configuration with an empty list of management Organizations or of signing roles fails to render, so no invalid policy is shipped and no test reports one as passing.

The rest of this ticket is the exact instruction for the model that does the work.

## 3. The model (the law)
1. **The Organization decides, not the tier alone.** The deny conditions keep their `all` item (the scope's Org differs from the requester's) and gain an `any` block of two items: the requester's org claim is `NotIn` `managementOrgs`, or the requester's tier claim is `NotIn` `allowedCrossOrgTiers`. A cross-Organization grant is admitted only when both claims are on their lists.
2. **The list is a value.** `userAccessBoundary.managementOrgs` in `platform/crossplane-claims/chart/values.yaml`, default `[openova-internal]`, rendered by a `range` like `allowedCrossOrgTiers`, so a Sovereign adds a partner Organization without a template edit.
3. **No empty list.** An empty `managementOrgs` or `allowedCrossOrgTiers` renders a condition whose `value` is null; the engine refuses such a policy and the Kyverno CLI v1.19.2 reports it as passing (field 4). The template therefore fails the render with `userAccessBoundary.<key> is empty`, and case 3f of `tests/composition-validate.sh` proves both.
4. **The copy is the render.** `tests/useraccess-boundary/policy.yaml` of the policy chart is the render of the claims chart's template with its defaults, the `# Source:` line and the first `---` removed, byte for byte (the rule CMP-066 set).
5. **Verdicts.** For the five resources of `resources.yaml`: developer of `acme` 1 pass, 2 fail, 2 skip (unchanged); owner of `acme` 1 pass (the grant inside `acme`), 2 fail (the grants into `other-corp`), 2 skip; owner of `openova-internal` 3 pass, 2 skip; developer of `openova-internal` 3 fail, 2 skip. Twenty tests.
6. **Nothing else changes.** The policy stays a legacy `ClusterPolicy` in the claims chart at `Audit`, its preconditions and message are unchanged, a requester without an org claim stays refused across Organizations, and the description of the check in `model/checks.yaml` already states the rule this ticket makes true.
