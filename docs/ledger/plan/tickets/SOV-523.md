# SOV-523 — A delivery of the release route resumes after one eviction of any of its Pods

## In plain words

**The problem today.** A delivery of a release to a Sovereign runs as Kubernetes Jobs that never replace a Pod. The platform drains nodes for maintenance every night, so a delivery whose Pod is on a drained node fails, and one region can be left on the new release while another is not.

**What changes.** Every Job of a delivery replaces one Pod that an eviction took, and no other. The new Pod resumes from what the delivery recorded, read with certainty: it does not stage, copy or push again what an earlier Pod finished, and a read or a write it cannot make stops it with the reason recorded, never counted as work not done.

**Done means:**

- A delivery whose Pod is evicted once, in its prewarm or in its promote, still ends delivered, with every region on the release and each region's promote recorded as before.
- When the replacement Pod cannot read the delivery's state, it stops, records why, and the delivery's state names the regions that moved and the ones that did not; it never says that nothing moved when a region did.
- When the prewarm cannot record that it finished, the delivery stops before any region moves, and the pull credential is deleted in every region.
- A follower region's result is read from the Pod that ran last, not from a Pod that an eviction took.
- A bootstrap Pod that replaces an evicted one stages nothing again once the delivery Job of its request exists, and waits for that Job instead.
- A Pod killed in another way, or a second eviction, still fails the delivery and is recorded as before.

The rest of this ticket is the exact instruction for the model that does the work.

## 2. End goal
A delivery through `PUT /api/v1/sovereign/release` on a Sovereign survives one eviction of any of its Pods: the bootstrap Job's (stage, deliver), the delivery Job's (prewarm, promote) and a follower region's Job (stage, check, promote, rollback). Each of these Jobs replaces exactly one Pod that a disruption took (the condition `DisruptionTarget`, which a drain's eviction sets) and no other; the new Pod resumes from state it read with certainty. A read or a write that the resume rests on is made again, or the Pod stops and the state key `resume` says why; a failure is never taken as "not done". A Job's end is read from its conditions and its result from the log of the Pod of its current attempt. A Pod killed otherwise, or a second eviction, fails the delivery and is recorded as today.

## 3. The model (the law)
1. One retry, for an eviction only. The bootstrap Job and the route's follower Job (`release_job.go`, `retryOneEviction`) and the delivery Job and its follower Jobs (`release-run.sh`, `RELEASE_RETRY`) carry the same three fields: `backoffLimit: 1`; `podFailurePolicy` with the rules, in this order, `Count` on the pod condition `DisruptionTarget` = `True` and `FailJob` on any exit code other than 0; `podReplacementPolicy: Failed`, so no new Pod starts before the old one is gone. Every exit of the release scripts, which record their own cause, fails the Job at once.
2. Certainty. `sure <file> <command...>` runs a call that a resume or a record rests on, repeats a failure up to `RELEASE_TRIES` (5) times `FOLLOWER_POLL_SECONDS` apart, and fails when no call answered. `status_must` sends its patch through `sure`. A Pod that cannot read what its resume rests on stops (`stop_pod`): it records `resume` ("a Pod of <job> stopped: <why>") and exits 1, and writes no phase over what an earlier Pod recorded.
3. The bootstrap's stage (not a dry run) reads, with `sure`, the delivery Job `<RELEASE_JOB>-delivery` of its request (`--ignore-not-found`). Found: an earlier Pod staged every region and that Job promotes what it recorded, so nothing is staged again (a new `release/<id>` would be pushed under that Job); the stage records `resume` and exits 0, and the bootstrap's deliver waits for that Job (`delivery_wait`, the Job as read) and then ends as today (credential removal in every region; `delivery_unrecorded` when the Job failed). Not found: the stage runs as today. Unreadable: the Pod stops; nothing is staged again or removed.
4. The prewarm reads the state with `sure`. `prewarmedBy` equal to its own Job: an earlier Pod completed the prewarm, so it records `resume` and exits 0. Absent: no Pod of this Job completed it, so none promoted and the credential is still in every region; the prewarm runs. After step 03 succeeds it records `prewarmedBy` with `status_must` BEFORE it deletes the credential; when that write fails, it deletes the credential, records phase `failed`, step `prewarm` ("nothing moved in any region", true because promote never began) and exits 1. The prewarm's secret reference to the pull Secret is `optional`, so a replacement Pod starts after the credential is gone.
5. Promote reads the state with `sure`. When it holds `promoteRegion`, an earlier Pod began the promote: promote keeps that record (it writes no `promoting` and no `promoteRegion ""`), and `promote_reach` records no region before the one that earlier Pod reached. It checks and promotes again; `release-region.sh` answers `promoted` where a main already is the staged commit. `<region>.promotedFrom` is the main the stage recorded (`<region>.from`, copied before any check), not a later answer.
6. A Job's end is its condition `Complete` or `Failed` (`job_end`), never `.status.failed`, which counts a replaced Pod while the Job runs on. Its result is the log of its newest Pod (`job_pod`; at one creation second, the Pod no disruption took); `kubectl logs job/<job>` reads the oldest. A follower log that cannot be read records why, and the region's result is then `failed`, never an answer. A follower Job deleted before a phase is applied again is deleted with `--cascade=foreground`, so its Pods are gone first.
7. Unchanged: `release-region.sh`, the release contract (`files/release/contract` stays `1`), the templates of the cutover chart, the RBAC, the route (`release.go`). The two copies of `release-run.sh` stay byte-identical. No Python is added; no Service and no port of any kind.
