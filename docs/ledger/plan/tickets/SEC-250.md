# SEC-250 — PKI engine, internal issuer and mesh issuer in the primary region and on a Sovereign of one region

## In plain words

**The problem today.** Inside a Sovereign nothing issues certificates for internal service names or for the mesh between regions: OpenBao holds no certificate authority, and cert-manager has no issuer that signs with one. A login role of OpenBao that names no audience also accepts any token of the account it binds, so an issuer that logs in must be held to an audience. The network policy of the OpenBao namespace lets in only a fixed list of namespaces, and cert-manager's is not on it.

**What changes.** OpenBao gets a root for the Sovereign and an intermediate for its region, both made once inside OpenBao, and cert-manager gets two issuers that sign with that intermediate, one for internal names and one for the mesh, and may now reach OpenBao. A secondary region gets the engine and its roles, but no root and no signing of its intermediate yet.

**Done means:**

- With the kit's values, the primary region and a Sovereign of one region mount the certificate engine and the root mount, write the two signing roles, and run a Job that makes the root and the region's intermediate.
- The Job makes the root and the intermediate only while they are missing, and treats a mount as empty only on the exact answer OpenBao's command gives for an empty list; any other answer stops it before any write. Its policy grants the four paths it writes and nothing else.
- A secondary region renders the engine, its two roles and cert-manager's role, and nothing that needs the primary region's OpenBao: no root mount, no Job and no bootstrap role. With the certificate engine on, an empty region role fails the render instead of becoming primary.
- cert-manager has the ClusterIssuers openbao-internal and sovereign-mesh-ca; each logs in with a token for the audience openbao, which OpenBao's role accepts, the same token without that audience is refused, and both roles sign the key a certificate gets when it names none.
- The network policy of the OpenBao namespace admits the namespace cert-manager runs in.
- With the chart's defaults nothing of the certificate authority renders.
- The gate check that internal certificates of both regions chain to one root is still not built: this task does not make it pass on two regions.

The rest of this ticket is the exact instruction for the model that does the work.

## 2. End goal
On a Sovereign of one region, and in the primary region of two, bp-openbao holds the Sovereign's one root in `pki-root/` and the region's intermediate in `pki/`, both generated inside OpenBao by Job `openbao-pki-bootstrap-<hash>`, and cert-manager signs certificates through two ClusterIssuers of bp-cert-manager-issuers: `openbao-internal` (role `internal-server`: names below `svc.cluster.local` and `cluster.local`, at most 30 days) and `sovereign-mesh-ca` (role `mesh`: any name, at most 90 days). Both log in at `auth/kubernetes` as role `cert-manager-pki`, which requires the audience `openbao`; the issuers add that audience to the token cert-manager asks for, so their login is accepted and a token without it is refused. NetworkPolicy `openbao-default-deny` of bp-plane-isolation admits the namespace `cert-manager`, so the issuers' requests reach OpenBao. Both roles sign every key cert-manager makes, the RSA 2048 key of a Certificate that names none included. A secondary region renders `pki/`, its two roles and role `cert-manager-pki`, and nothing that needs the primary region's OpenBao; signing its intermediate is SEC-252's, over the path spike SEC-251 names, so gate check SEC-G09 does not pass on two regions with this package.

## 3. The model (the law)
1. bp-openbao takes the value `pki`: `enabled` (default `false`), `regionRole` (`primary` or `secondary`, chart default `primary`) and `domain` (the Sovereign's domain, in the common names). With `pki.enabled`, a `regionRole` that is neither `primary` nor `secondary`, the empty string included, fails the render with `pki.regionRole: "<value>" is neither primary nor secondary`: an empty role is never read as primary. The PKI is on when `pki.enabled` and `kubernetesAuth.enabled` are both true; one helper, `openbao.pki` in `templates/_config.tpl`, decides it for every template. Kit slot 08 sets `enabled: true`, `regionRole: ${SOVEREIGN_REGION_ROLE:=primary}` (the variable and default that slots 01, 16b and 16c already use) and `domain: ${SOVEREIGN_FQDN}`.
2. In every region the desired state of the configuration reconciler holds, in this order of ops: `secrets-mount sys/mounts/pki` (type `pki`, `max_lease_ttl` `8760h`) before the roles of `pki/`; policy `pki-sign-internal` (create and update on `pki/sign/internal-server` and `pki/sign/mesh`, nothing else) before auth role `cert-manager-pki` (service account `cert-manager` in namespace `cert-manager`, policy `pki-sign-internal`, 15 minutes, at most 1 hour, audience `openbao`); role `pki/roles/internal-server` (allowed domains `svc.cluster.local` and `cluster.local` with subdomains, at most `720h`) and role `pki/roles/mesh` (any name, at most `2160h`), both server and client certificates, `key_type` `any`, no common name required.
3. The key. A ClusterIssuer has no field for the key: each Certificate names one, and one that names none gets cert-manager's default, RSA 2048. So the roles are the side that agrees: with `key_type` `any` OpenBao signs RSA of at least 2048 bits, EC of at least P-224 and Ed25519, which covers every key cert-manager makes; ECDSA P-256 is the size a Certificate asks for, and the ClusterIssuer template says so.
4. In the primary region only, also: `secrets-mount sys/mounts/pki-root` (`max_lease_ttl` `87600h`) before the roles of `pki/`; policy `pki-bootstrap` (create and update on exactly `pki-root/root/generate/internal`, `pki-root/root/sign-intermediate`, `pki/intermediate/generate/internal` and `pki/intermediate/set-signed`; no glob, so no `generate/exported` and no `cross-sign`) before auth role `pki-bootstrap` (service account `openbao-pki-bootstrap` in the release namespace, audience `openbao`); and the ServiceAccount, ConfigMap `openbao-pki-bootstrap-script` and Job `openbao-pki-bootstrap-<10 hex>` of `templates/pki-bootstrap-job.yaml`. The hash covers the script, its environment and the image (the OpenBao server image of the chart), so a change of any of them runs the Job again.
5. The Job logs in as `pki-bootstrap` (waiting at most 600 seconds for the reconciler to bind the role), then: when `pki-root/` lists no issuer it generates the root `<domain> root` (EC P-384, `87600h`); when `pki/` lists no issuer it generates the region's key and signing request in `pki/` (`<domain> <region> intermediate`, EC P-256), has the root sign it (`8760h`) and sets the signed certificate alone in `pki/`, without the root. A mount lists no issuer only when `bao list <mount>/issuers` exits 2 with the line `No value found at <mount>/issuers`, the answer of the OpenBao 2.7.2 command to an empty list (field 4); any other answer ends the Job with an error before any write, so a refusal is never read as an empty mount and a second root is never made. The Job sets `BAO_CLI_NO_COLOR=1`. The lists of `issuers/` need no grant: the PKI engine serves them without a token check. No key leaves OpenBao.
6. A secondary region renders no line that names `pki-root` or `pki-bootstrap` and no Job: its `pki/` holds no intermediate, so OpenBao refuses both issuers' requests there until SEC-252.
7. The reconciler's own grant (`openbao-config-admin`) gains exactly `sys/mounts/pki`, in the primary region `sys/mounts/pki-root`, and `pki/roles/*` (create, update, read). It writes the roles of `pki/`, never a key, an issuer or a certificate.
8. bp-cert-manager-issuers renders, in a new template, the ClusterIssuers `openbao-internal` (path `pki/sign/internal-server`) and `sovereign-mesh-ca` (path `pki/sign/mesh`) on `http://openbao.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8200`, Kubernetes auth at `/v1/auth/kubernetes`, role `cert-manager-pki`, `serviceAccountRef` `cert-manager` with `audiences: [openbao]`; and Role and RoleBinding `cert-manager-openbao-token` in the release namespace (kit slot 02a: `cert-manager`), which let cert-manager create a token of its own service account and nothing else. They are not for public names: Gateway certificates still come from the ACME issuers of `templates/clusterissuers.yaml`. The function test of the chart waits only for its ACME ClusterIssuers, and its two comments that said "every ClusterIssuer" now say "every ACME ClusterIssuer".
9. bp-plane-isolation: the `openbao` entry of `components` gains `cert-manager` in `allowIngressFrom`, so `openbao-default-deny` admits the namespace of kit slot 02, where cert-manager's controller dials OpenBao. Kit slot 26b sets no `components`, so a Sovereign gets the chart's list. The new test `tests/openbao-ingress.sh` of the chart checks it and shows that its check fails without the entry.
10. The audience. Under OpenBao 2.7.2 (the server image the chart pins) a Kubernetes auth role that names no audience accepts any token of the account it binds, and a role that names one accepts only a token that carries it. cert-manager's token for a ClusterIssuer carries `vault://<issuer>` and the audiences of `serviceAccountRef.audiences`. So both auth roles require `openbao`, and both issuers list it. The test applies that rule to the rendered role and issuers; no OpenBao runs.
11. With the chart's defaults nothing of the PKI renders. No chart version line changes, `docs/ledger/gates.yaml` does not change, no new Python, no change to a live system. The intermediate is not renewed by this package.
