# PRD-061 result: vector search with the readable principals on the chunk row

Date: 2026-11-19, one session, lab branch on origin/main 92d59775d9. Type: lab test.
Extension tested: `vector` 0.8.6 in the chart's image (PostgreSQL 16.15), the same image digest as PRD-060.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| The readers rule gives the rows of the PRD-060 rule | Yes. 11 principal lists over one million chunks, 0 rows differ. |
| Source-grant reader (p1), the design's transaction | Recall at 10 is 1.0. Warm p95 is 74.7 ms. Holds. |
| Per-document reader (p2), the design's transaction | Recall at 10 is 0.9996. Warm p95 is 107.6 ms. Holds, but only through the plan cache (see below). |
| The same transaction with the principals also bound in the WHERE clause | p1 1.0 at 76.9 ms, p2 1.0 at 106.1 ms. Holds under custom and generic plans. |

| A grant change (supplementary) | Rewrites `readers` of every chunk it covers: 2.5 to 4.1 ms per chunk warm. None of the updates is HOT. |

Both reader shapes meet the bar (recall at 10 of at least 0.99, warm p95 under 200 ms), so the outcome is holds and PRD-202 builds this layout. The search that holds is not the HNSW index. It is an exact scan of the reader's rows found through the GIN index on `readers`, followed by a top-10 sort. For the per-document reader the planner chooses that scan only when the principals are also bound in the WHERE clause, or when it falls back to a generic plan. PRD-202 must therefore bind them.

## The question

1. With the readable principals on the chunk row (`readers text[]`, a GIN index, and a chunk policy that tests only that column), does the search hold recall at 10 of at least 0.99 against an exact scan, with a warm p95 under 200 ms for the whole transaction?
2. The test conditions are those of PRD-060: one million real 1,024-dimension chunks, and two readers who each read one percent of documents. p1 reads through source grants. p2 reads only through per-document reader lists.
3. The bar and the two readers were fixed before any run. The judged number is pass 2 of the design's transaction, with the plan the planner picks.

    - **hnsw**: `SET LOCAL enable_sort = off` and `enable_incremental_sort = off`. Only the ordered HNSW scan avoids a sort.

- **Sweep (supplementary, not judged).** Group readers of 1, 5, 10 and 25 % of sources (`group:g00` to `group:g24`), with 200 queries each.

### Which plan each reader got (run 2)

| Reader | Statement | Plan of the 20 explained queries (custom plan) | Estimated rows | Plan cache in the passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p1 | design | 20 of 20 `Limit -> Sort -> Bitmap Heap Scan -> BitmapAnd (chunk_readers, chunk_source)` | 99 | 5 custom, 990 generic |
| p1 | bound | the same, 20 of 20 | 1 | 5 custom, 990 generic |
| p2 | design | 20 of 20 `Limit -> Index Scan using chunk_embedding` (HNSW) | 7,554 | 5 custom, 990 generic |
| p2 | bound | 20 of 20 `Limit -> Sort -> Bitmap Heap Scan (chunk_readers)` | 73 | 5 custom, 990 generic |

- **Why p2's design pass holds.** In each pass the first five runs used custom plans, which took the HNSW scan. Those five give the three queries out of order and the recall of 0.9996. The other 990 runs used the generic plan, which is the exact scan.
- **Without that fallback.** A connection that does not prepare statements, or a pool that drops prepared statements, gets custom plans. For p2 that is the HNSW path, whose measured recall is 0.9266.
- **Why the planner chooses so.** With the values known, it estimates the source filter (788 of 1,000 sources) at 79 % and the readers condition at about 1 %, so 7,554 rows. At that estimate the HNSW scan under `LIMIT 10` is costed at 8,923, below the exact scan (26,049 in the bound plan). Binding the principals counts the readers condition twice: 73 rows estimated. The HNSW scan would then have to read far more of the index to find 10 rows, and the exact scan wins.
- **The bound condition stays a filter.** `arrayoverlap` is not leakproof, so the bound condition is never an index condition. In every exact plan the GIN index condition is the policy's own `readers && current_setting(...)`.
- **The exact scan of p1** read 10,110 rows from 2,152 heap blocks, with 89,799 shared buffer hits and none read from disk. It ran 74.8 ms. Nothing was removed by a filter.
- **The explained HNSW plan of p2** read a median of 8,146 blocks from disk. It removed about 1,000 rows per query by the filter.

## Answer

- **Holds for both reader shapes.** With `readers` on the chunk row, the source-grant reader gets recall 1.0 at a warm p95 of 74.7 ms. The per-document reader gets 0.9996 at 107.6 ms with the design's statement, and 1.0 at 106.1 ms with the principals bound. The PRD-060 failure of the per-document reader is gone: the policy no longer reads `document`, `source_acl` or `document_acl`.
- **The search that holds is exact.** For a one-percent reader the best plan does not use the HNSW index. It finds the reader's rows through the GIN index (with the source btree when the source filter is narrow), computes the distance for each, and keeps the top 10. No HNSW setting tried reaches 0.99 at one percent; the best was 0.948.
- **The design's statement alone is not enough.** Whether the per-document reader gets the exact scan depends on the plan cache. Binding the principals in the WHERE clause gives the exact scan under custom and generic plans. Access is then checked inside the query with bound values, and again by row-level security.
- **The limit is breadth.** The exact scan costs about 7 to 11 µs per readable chunk, so it meets 200 ms up to about 2 % of a million chunks. Above that, neither path meets both bars. At 5 % and 10 % the exact scan takes 366 to 770 ms. At 25 % the planner takes the HNSW scan, which is fast but has recall 0.885.

## What later stories must do

- **PRD-202 (store) builds this layout.**
  - Add `chunk.readers text[] NOT NULL` with a GIN index.
  - The chunk read policy is `readers && current_setting('cortex.principals')::text[]`.
  - `readers` holds the source's principals when the document inherits, otherwise the document's own list plus the source's managers. This was measured equal to the PRD-060 rule.
- **The search statement binds the principals:** `AND c.readers && $5` with the caller's principal list, in addition to the policy.
  - The measured alternative is `SET LOCAL enable_indexscan = off` per transaction (exact for both readers: 1.0 at 74.1 and 106.2 ms). It also takes the exact scan for broad readers, where its cost grows with the readable chunks.
  - Either way, PRD-202 should pin the plan with a test on a database: explain the statement for a narrow per-document reader, with enough rows for the planner to use the GIN index, and assert a plan without `chunk_embedding`.
- **The rules measured by PRD-060 stand:**
  - the document policy tests its own columns;
  - `SET LOCAL hnsw.iterative_scan = relaxed_order` is set per transaction, because broad readers use the HNSW scan;
  - the store re-sorts, because the HNSW plans returned rows out of order in 338 to 351 of 1,000 queries;
  - the store checks `extversion` at start.
- **The store states the breadth limit.** The bar holds for readers whose readable chunks in one store are below about 20,000. That is measured as 2 % of a million chunks of this size on a 4-vCPU runner. For broader readers the breadth table gives the measured numbers. The bar for them is not set: it is a decision for the architect, and the measured options are in the breadth table.
- **A grant change is a write of every chunk it covers.**
  - Every path that changes a grant (a source's readers, or a document's own list) must rewrite `readers` of the chunks it covers. PRD-202 provides that rewrite in the store; it runs as a job, so that search and the write do not wait on each other.
  - The change is visible to search when its transaction commits: 39 s for 9,594 chunks in one transaction.
  - None of these updates is HOT, so the table needs vacuum after large grant changes: 12 s for 11,892 rows here.

## What later tasks must take into account

These were not measured, and they need a Sovereign with the product or a later lab test:

- An instance managed by CloudNativePG on the platform's cloud volumes. All runs used the runner's local disk, with the index and the reader's rows in memory.
- Concurrent searches, and searches during a grant change. All runs used one connection at a time.
- Readers with many principals, for example a user in many groups. Here a reader had at most two principals.
- More than one million chunks per store, and the embedding model PRD-080 chooses. The exact scan's cost follows the reader's chunk count, not the corpus size.
- Grants that follow topic. Here grants were assigned by hash, so a reader's documents are spread at random through the vector space.
