# SEC-251 result: how a secondary region's OpenBao has its intermediate signed by the primary

Date: 2026-11-27, one session, on origin/main f2b2bf76fc. Type: spike. Outcome: **a name per region** (candidate 2). The peer path over the cluster mesh (candidate 1) is refused: on a fresh install of two regions its first signing waits for the mesh, and the mesh waits for that signing.

Every answer says how it was found:
- **read**: source of this repository, or upstream source;
- **rendered**: `helm template` of the chart on this branch;
- **run**: two OpenBao 2.7.2 servers on this host (section "Measured in the local run").

No cluster of ours was used, not even to read. Every script ran with `KUBECONFIG=/dev/null`. No node port anywhere.

## Summary

| | Candidate 1: peer path over the mesh | Candidate 2: a name per region |
|---|---|---|
| First signing, fresh install of two regions | Never completes once the mesh takes its certificates from `sovereign-mesh-ca`. The call needs the mesh; the mesh needs the secondary's intermediate | Completes. It needs the two regions' gateways only |
| Renewal | Works while the mesh is up. If the intermediate lapses, the mesh cannot come back, and the renewal path is gone with it | Works whenever both regions' gateways answer, whatever the state of the mesh |
| Added by the provisioning | Nothing | One address per region that reaches only that region's gateway, and one DNS record per region |
| Primary down | The secondary keeps issuing leaves from its intermediate (run); no renewal | The same (run) |
| Accepted records | Against ADR-0018 S13: "never over the mesh" | Meets ADR-0018 S13 and the target model of ADR-0026 point 5 |

## The question

Each region runs its own OpenBao. The root is in the primary region. Each region holds an intermediate signed by that root, and its issuers `openbao-internal` and `sovereign-mesh-ca` sign from it (SEC-250, SEC-252). By what path does a secondary region send its signing request to the primary, and get the signed certificate back, at first install and at every renewal? Two candidates were judged:
1. The existing peer path: the peer trust of the OpenBao chart, over the cluster mesh.
2. A name per region for OpenBao.

Refused in advance: the parent carrying the signing request during provisioning. A renewal after the hand-over would need the parent again.

## What exists today (both candidates)

It also says: "A region's signing request travels once over the north-south gateway and never over the mesh." On main no PKI exists yet. The render of the chart for two regions holds no line with `pki` (rendered).

**T2. The mesh will take its certificates from the PKI.** The hermetic mesh test lays the mesh layer with the certificates of the mesh API server from ClusterIssuer `sovereign-mesh-ca` (tests/hermetic/mesh/mesh-test.sh lines 126 to 152; plan entry NET-120) (read). Its case B, one root with an intermediate per cluster, passed in four runs of workflow test-mesh-hermetic (read: their job summaries):
- 44873374839 on main, b184ef8f4f, 21 Nov;
- 44880162482 and 44923596128, 21 Nov;
- 45943329254, 27 Nov.

NET-131 ("Mesh layer from Git with the shared authority") is planned and blocked. Today the mesh's trust is something else: catalyst-api reads each region's `cilium-ca` Secret and copies it to the peers (products/catalyst/bootstrap/api/internal/handler/clustermesh.go lines 1198 to 1216 and line 1341) (read).

**T3. The public name `bao.<fqdn>` lands in either region** (read). This is what SEC-250's developer measured.

**T4. Peer trust today runs over the mesh** (read, rendered).
- Each region renders a mesh-global Service `openbao-<self>` (platform/openbao/chart/templates/peer-trust.yaml lines 33 to 57) and a stub `openbao-<peer>` per peer (lines 62 to 81). The names are `openbao-<region>.openbao.svc.cluster.local` (templates/_helpers.tpl lines 46 to 52).
- Each region's identity issuer is its mesh name: `http://openbao-<self>.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8200` (templates/_config.tpl line 468). Key `openbao-peer-push`: RS256, rotated every 24 hours (line 469).
- Each peer gets a mount `jwt-peer-<peer>` whose `oidc_discovery_url` is the peer's issuer over the mesh (_config.tpl lines 476 to 482). Rendered for region `a` of `["a","b"]`: `auth.jwt-peer-b.config.json: {"oidc_discovery_url":"http://openbao-b.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8200/v1/identity/oidc"}`, and region a's issuer `http://openbao-a.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8200`.

- The birth Job of platform/secrets-lib mints its identity token of role `PEER_TOKEN_ROLE` and logs in at the peer's `auth/jwt-peer-<self>` as `PEER_ROLE` (templates/_birth.tpl lines 140 to 148; its environment: templates/_generated-secrets.tpl lines 295 and 296).

**T5. Egress from the openbao namespace is open.** Plane isolation's default deny admits egress to every pod and to 0.0.0.0/0 (platform/plane-isolation/chart/templates/default-deny-networkpolicy.yaml lines 85 to 113) (read).

**T6. The gateway certificate names `*.${SOVEREIGN_FQDN}` and the apex only** (clusters/_template/sovereign-tls/cilium-gateway-cert.yaml lines 106 and 107). A one-label name `bao-<region>.<fqdn>` is covered. A two-label name `bao.<region>.<fqdn>` is not (read).

**T7. Per-region addresses** (read).

## Candidate 1: the peer path over the cluster mesh

**What exists.** Everything in T4, rendered only when the registry names more than one region.

**A signing request, step by step.** Secondary B, primary A.
1. B's PKI Job (SEC-250) writes `pki/intermediate/generate/internal` and gets a CSR. The key stays in B.
2. It mints B's identity token of a new identity role, for example `openbao-pki` (audience `openbao-pki`, claim `openova_region=b`).
3. It calls `http://openbao-a.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8200/v1/auth/jwt-peer-b/login` with a new role. B's stub Service sends the call over the mesh to A's active OpenBao.
4. A checks the token with B's public keys, fetched from B's discovery URL over the mesh (_config.tpl line 481).
5. With the token it gets back, the Job writes `pki-root/root/sign-intermediate` on A, then `pki/intermediate/set-signed` on B.

The chart would add, on the primary: a role and a policy on each `jwt-peer-<peer>`, the new audience in the key's `allowed_client_ids` (_config.tpl line 469), and the PKI Job's label in the ingress policy (peer-trust.yaml lines 116 to 137).

**A renewal.** The same five steps, once a year, while the mesh is up.

**What the provisioning must add.** Nothing.

**When the primary is down.** The stub Service has no backend, so step 3 fails and the Job retries. B issues leaves from its intermediate (run, M7). One more failure is particular to this candidate. Suppose B's intermediate lapses before A comes back. B can then issue no mesh certificate, its mesh certificates expire, and the mesh cannot reconnect. Step 3 cannot reach A even after A is back, so the situation needs a hand action.

**Why it fails.** Take a fresh install of two regions after NET-131. B's mesh API server gets its certificates only from B's `sovereign-mesh-ca` (T2). That issuer signs from B's intermediate. B's intermediate needs step 3, and step 3 needs the mesh. None of the three can start first. Candidate 1 would work on main today only because the mesh's trust is still the copied `cilium-ca` (T2), which the shape of NET-120 and NET-131 replaces. It also contradicts ADR-0018 S13 (T1).

**Packages that would change.** platform/openbao/chart: _config.tpl, values.yaml, peer-trust.yaml, pki-bootstrap-job.yaml and their tests. NET-131 would also have to keep a mesh authority outside the PKI.

## Candidate 2: a name per region

**What exists.**
- No per-region name for OpenBao. Only `bao.<fqdn>`, which lands in either region (T3).
- The certificate covers one-label names (T6).
- Cilium's Gateway API translation puts both `host` and `host:*` into every virtual host's domains (upstream Cilium v1.19.3, commit f5eb641b18, operator/pkg/model/translation/envoy_virtual_host.go lines 143 to 154). So a call that names a port still matches its route (read, upstream).
- Egress is open (T5). The gateway already reaches OpenBao for `bao.<fqdn>`.
- A per-region address exists on the local pack. On the reference cloud it comes with NET-184 (T7).
- DNS records are written one per name, at provisioning (T3).

**The name.** `bao-<region-id>.<sovereign-fqdn>`. The region id is the region's canonical id (`peerTrust.selfRegion`; DNS-label checked by _helpers.tpl lines 36 to 38). It is one label, so the existing certificate covers it (T6).

Each region's chart renders an HTTPRoute for its own name only, with three paths: `/v1/identity/oidc/.well-known/keys` in every region, and on the primary also `/v1/auth/jwt-pki-<peer>/login` and `/v1/pki-root/root/sign-intermediate`. The DNS record points the name at that region's own edge address. When NET-200 adds its wildcard record, this explicit name still wins over it.

**A signing request, step by step** (run, M1 to M4).
1. B writes `pki/intermediate/generate/internal` and gets a CSR. The key stays in B. Run: a CSR of 378 bytes.
2. B's Job mints its identity token of role `openbao-pki`: audience `openbao-pki`, claim `openova_region=b`, 5 minutes. Run, the claims: `{"aud":"openbao-pki","iss":"http://openbao-b.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8300/v1/identity/oidc","openova_region":"b",...}`.
3. It logs in at `https://bao-a.<fqdn>/v1/auth/jwt-pki-b/login` with role `pki-sign`. Only A's gateway answers that name.
4. A's mount `jwt-pki-b` checks the signature with B's public keys from `jwks_url=https://bao-b.<fqdn>/v1/identity/oidc/.well-known/keys`. It checks the issuer string against `bound_issuer`, which fetches nothing. Run:
   - login accepted;
   - a token whose claim names another region refused: `claim "openova_region" does not match any associated bound claim values`;
   - a token with audience `openbao-peer-push` refused: `invalid audience (aud) claim`;
   - a damaged token refused.
5. The login token carries policy `pki-sign-intermediate-b`: `update` on `pki-root/root/sign-intermediate` only, and `allowed_parameters` that admit any `csr`, the one `common_name` and `ttl=8760h`. It lives 5 minutes and has one use. Run:
   - the right name signed;
   - another `common_name` refused with 403;
   - a second use of the same token refused with 403;
   - `sys/mounts`, `pki-root/root/generate/internal`, `pki-root/issue/any` and `identity/oidc/key/openbao-peer-push` refused with 403;
   - unauthenticated PKI reads (`LIST pki-root/issuers`, the CA certificate) answer, and do not use up the token: the signing call after them succeeded, and the token was gone after it (`lookup-self`: 403).
6. B writes `pki/intermediate/set-signed`. Run:
   - accepted, and on the first signing the imported issuer is B's default issuer;
   - a leaf from B's role verifies against the root: `openssl verify -CAfile root.pem -untrusted int.pem leaf.pem` printed OK;
   - the leaf's `issuing_ca` is B's intermediate, byte for byte.

Why `jwks_url` and not `oidc_discovery_url` (run, M1). A discovery URL whose host is not the issuer's host is refused: `issuer did not match the returned issuer, expected "http://bao-b.sov.example:8300/v1/identity/oidc" got "http://openbao-b.openbao.svc.cluster.local:8300/v1/identity/oidc"`. B's issuer stays its mesh name, because the peer push and the birth Jobs log in with it (T4). A second mount with `jwks_url` and `bound_issuer` leaves them unchanged (M2).

**A renewal** (run, M5 and M6). The same six steps, run by a schedule inside B: a CronJob, needing neither Git nor the parent. It runs when less than half the intermediate's life remains. Step 1 makes a new key each time.
- `set-signed` adds the new issuer but does not move the default (M5). `default` stayed on the first issuer, and `default_follows_latest_issuer` is `false`. A leaf issued after `set-signed` still came from the old intermediate. Only after `pki/config/issuers default=<new id>` did leaves come from the new one. Leaves of the old intermediate still verify against the root. So the renewal must write the default itself.
- A leaf asked to outlive its intermediate is refused by default (M6). `leaf_not_after_behavior` is `err`, and the answer reads `cannot satisfy request, as TTL would result in notAfter of ... that is beyond the expiration of the CA certificate`. With `truncate` the leaf is issued, cut to the intermediate's end.
- So a renewal must finish while the intermediate has more life left than the longest leaf the region's roles issue. Example: an intermediate of one year, renewed at half its life, with leaves of at most 90 days. The primary can then be away for about 92 days before any leaf is refused.
- Keep `err` and renew at half the life. A truncated leaf hides the coming end of the intermediate, while `err` turns it into a certificate that is not Ready, which an alert sees.

**What the provisioning must add.**
- **P1.** Per region, an address that reaches only that region's gateway: `regions[].edgeAddress` of the network registry. The local pack has it (T7). The reference cloud gets it with NET-184. Until then it has no such address, and SEC-G09 cannot pass there.
- **P2.** One DNS record per region: `bao-<region>.<fqdn>`, pointing at that region's edge address. Two writers fit: the provisioning's record step (sovereign_dns_records.go, which today points a fixed list of names at one address) or NET-200's record hook. A record is written once, and a renewal never needs the parent, so this is not the refused shape. Not traced: which writer adds the record of a region added after the hand-over.
- **P3.** Nothing in the node start-up data. The names come from `SOVEREIGN_FQDN` and the region ids that slot 08 already receives (08-openbao.yaml lines 262, 335 and 336). The id of the root holder must be a constant of the first boot (T8), not the current writer.

**When the primary is down** (run, M7). B keeps issuing leaves from its intermediate: a leaf was issued with A stopped. The login at A fails (`connection refused`), and the CronJob tries again on its next run. When A is back, the exchange works whatever the state of the mesh.

A primary that never comes back is the same for both candidates. The root exists only in A's OpenBao storage. Restoring A's raft snapshot (SEC-180: `s3://<bucket>/<prefix><region>/`, platform/openbao/chart/values.yaml lines 315 to 317) brings it back. Otherwise every region gets a new root. A promotion under ADR-0027 moves the writer, not the root.

**Packages that would change.**
- platform/openbao/chart:
  - values.yaml: `pki.*`;
  - _config.tpl: identity role `openbao-pki` in every region; on the primary, per peer, a mount `jwt-pki-<peer>` with `jwks_url` and `bound_issuer`, role `pki-sign` and policy `pki-sign-intermediate-<peer>`;
  - httproute.yaml: the per-region host with its three paths;
  - pki-bootstrap-job.yaml: the remote signing, the default issuer and the renewal schedule;
  - tests: pki-render.sh and httproute-render.sh.
- products/catalyst/bootstrap/api/internal/handler/sovereign_dns_records.go, or NET-200's hook: the per-region records.
- tools/guards/cmd/edge-hosts: it fails on a hostname that the primary routes and the secondary does not (main.go lines 1 to 60). `bao-<region>` is routed in its own region only, by design, so the guard needs that exception.
- infra/providers/huawei: the per-region balancer of NET-184, not SEC-252.

## Answer

Recommended path: **candidate 2, a name per region.** Each region's OpenBao answers `bao-<region>.<fqdn>` through that region's own gateway. The secondary's Job logs in at the primary's name with its own identity token. The primary checks the token with the secondary's keys, fetched from the secondary's name. A one-use, five-minute token signs exactly one intermediate. Renewal is the same exchange, from a schedule in the secondary. On the reference cloud SEC-252 follows NET-184, which gives each region its own load balancer and edge address. On the local pack it can pass before.

Reasons:
1. It meets ADR-0018 S13 as written: over the north-south gateway, never over the mesh.
2. It has no circular dependency with the mesh. It works on a fresh install after NET-131, and after a lapse of the mesh.
3. It reuses what exists: the gateway, its certificate, the region ids already in slot 08, and the identity issuer and keys of peer trust. The existing peer push and birth Jobs do not change.
4. It needs the parent only for one durable DNS record per region, never for a signing or a renewal.
5. Its per-region address is the target model of ADR-0026 point 5, built by NET-184. The plan adds nothing that a later package removes.

Not chosen:
- **A listener per region on today's shared balancer.** The host stays `bao.<fqdn>`, a port per region picks the region, and Cilium matches the host with its port (read). It needs no new name, record or certificate. It is the measured option if the architect wants the two-region check on the reference cloud before NET-184. It adds, per region, a listener, a pool, members at host port 443 and a monitor, all of which NET-184 removes again.
- **Moving every region's issuer to its per-region name.** All peer trust would then leave the mesh. That is larger than this question needs.
- **The parent carrying the request.** Refused in advance.

## What follows for the plan

- **SEC-252** takes candidate 2 and these inputs:
  - the per-region host and its three paths;
  - the identity role `openbao-pki`;
  - the primary's mount `jwt-pki-<peer>` with `jwks_url` and `bound_issuer`;
  - policy `pki-sign-intermediate-<peer>` with `allowed_parameters`;
  - token TTL 5 minutes and one use;
  - the explicit default issuer after each `set-signed`;
  - the renewal CronJob at half the intermediate's life.
- **SEC-252's touches** gain platform/openbao/chart/templates/httproute.yaml, platform/openbao/chart/tests/httproute-render.sh, the record writer (sovereign_dns_records.go and its test, or NET-200's hook) and tools/guards/cmd/edge-hosts.
- **SEC-252's dependencies.** It depends on NET-184 for the reference cloud. SEC-G09 runs in `local` and `cloud` (docs/ledger/gates.yaml line 2190).
- **SEC-250.** The root holder (`pki.primary`) is a constant of the first boot.

## What later tasks must take into account

- **The ca.crt of a leaf.** ACC-030 measured that the bundle given to `set-signed` decides what cert-manager puts in `ca.crt` (docs/ledger/plan/spikes/ACC-030.md lines 124 to 128). If `ca.crt` is the intermediate, it changes at every renewal, because a renewal makes a new intermediate. If it is the root, it does not change. SEC-G09 compares them, so SEC-252 must choose with that in mind.
- **Trust of the gateway certificate.** The primary's OpenBao and the secondary's Job verify the gateway certificate with their own CA bundles. A Sovereign that runs on the staging issuer needs its staging root given to both (`jwks_ca_pem`, `BAO_CACERT`). Not run.
- **Not measured.** A call from a pod to its own cloud's balancer address, on the reference cloud. SEC-252 measures it from a pod in each region: the per-region name must answer with that region's OpenBao.
- **Not traced.** How a region added after the hand-over gets its kit while the mesh is not yet up. Follower regions fetch Git over the mesh (clusters/_template/sovereign-region/multi-region-follower/kustomization.yaml).
